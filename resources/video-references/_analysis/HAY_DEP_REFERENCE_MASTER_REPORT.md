## 4. Composition Patterns

The 5 reference videos exhibit a highly disciplined, tri-zonal vertical structure:
1. **Top Zone (0% – 22%)**: Topic Hook / Anchor Title.
   - Fixed position near top (y ~ 120px to 380px).
   - High-contrast typography (Yellow, Glowing Gold, or Stark White), 1 to 2 lines.
   - Remains completely stable, anchoring viewer retention as users scroll TikTok/Reels.
2. **Center Visual Zone (22% – 72%)**: The Core Narrative Illustration.
   - Artwork occupies 40% to 55% of total vertical canvas height.
   - Framed either as a clean floating vignette (Ref 4, Ref 5) or inside an organic parchment/chalkboard card (Ref 1, Ref 2, Ref 3).
   - Never stretched or distorted to full 9:16 bleed because human emotional scenes benefit from clear focus without edge clutter.
3. **Bottom Zone (72% – 100%)**: Narration Subtitle & Channel Identity.
   - 1-2 lines of subtitles in clean white sans-serif with subtle dark stroke/shadow.
   - Positioned around y = 80% to 88%.
   - Channel logo / watermark placed below subtitles (y ~ 90% to 94%) or as a subtle watermark.

---

## 5. Subject-Scale Patterns

- **Wide Shots**: Subject height occupies ~25% to 35% of the visual window height.
- **Medium Shots**: Subject height occupies ~45% to 65% of the visual window height.
- **Close / Portrait Shots**: Subject face and bust occupies ~70% to 85% of the visual window height.
- **Detail Shots**: Object occupies ~60% to 80% of the visual window (e.g. clock, bowl, hands).

---

## 6. Image-Occupancy Patterns

- **Actual Canvas Occupancy by Artwork**: **38% to 52%** of total screen area across all 5 reference videos.
- **Canvas Negative Space**: **48% to 62%**, thoughtfully utilized for:
  - Top anchor headline (creating scroll-stopping hook and topic retention).
  - Bottom caption readability (no clash with intricate artwork details).
  - Atmospheric ambient mood (parchment texture, dust motes, chalkboard dust, starry bokeh).

---

## 7. Typography Rules

1. **Persistent Top Hook Title**: All 5 reference videos maintain a persistent topic headline at the top. This is a foundational TikTok/Shorts platform retention grammar.
2. **Typography Hierarchy**:
   - Top Title: 52–68px equivalent, Bold, high contrast (Yellow with dark outline, Gold glow, or Chalk White).
   - Subtitles: 36–44px equivalent, Medium/SemiBold, centered, max 8–12 words per line.
3. **Caption Synchronicity**: Subtitles change precisely on spoken phrase boundaries (1.5s – 3.0s).

---

## 8. Motion Rules

- **Primary Motion Vector**: Hard visual cuts to new artwork every 2.0 – 4.0 seconds.
- **In-Shot Micro-Motion**: Very restrained (0.5% – 2% scale drift or slow 4–8px pan) to keep the drawing feeling alive without distracting from the emotional message.
- **Particle Dynamics**: Ambient atmospheric floating particles (warm dust motes in Ref 2, stars/snow in Ref 4, glowing embers in Ref 5) run continuously, tying the entire video together into a cohesive universe.

---

## 9. Transition Rules

- **Standard Transition**: **Hard Cut** (approx. 90% of transitions). Instant cut from one shot to the next maintains forward momentum and freshness.
- **Emotional Transition**: **Soft Dissolve / Crossfade** (approx. 10%), reserved for memory shifts or time lapses.
- **No Extraneous Wipes / Glitches**: Zero sliding, spinning, or 3D flipping.

---

## 10. Shot-Sequence Grammar

References use deliberate narrative shot sentences:
- **Sequence Pattern A (Domestic Empathy - Ref 2)**: WIDE (Couple at home) -> MEDIUM (Husband carrying groceries) -> MEDIUM (Husband fixing lightbulb) -> CLOSE (Sharing food at table) -> DETAIL (Holding hands).
- **Sequence Pattern B (Reflective Realization - Ref 4)**: WIDE (Family dinner table with warm light) -> MEDIUM (Husband & wife embracing child) -> MEDIUM (Man alone on bench) -> WIDE (Walking together in rain under umbrella) -> CLOSE (Quiet smile).
- **Sequence Pattern C (Parenting Cause-and-Effect - Ref 1)**: MEDIUM (Spoiling child on sofa) -> MEDIUM (Tying child shoes) -> DETAIL (Alarm clock ringing) -> MEDIUM (Child waking up on own) -> WIDE (Future independent child in mirror).

---

## 11. Visual Storytelling Rules

1. **Show Emotional Action, Not Just Characters Standing**: Characters are always engaged in a meaningful domestic gesture (eating together, passing food, carrying groceries, fixing appliances, sharing an umbrella, comforting a partner).
2. **Lighting Tells the Mood**:
   - Warm golden pools of light = safety, love, togetherness, gratitude.
   - Cold dark blue/grey exterior = hardship, loneliness, external pressure.
3. **Metaphorical Inserts**: A single clock, an umbrella in rain, a bowl of rice, or two hands holding convey more emotion than complex multi-character crowds.

---

## 12. Things References NEVER Do

1. **NEVER use generic corporate flat vector art or featureless stick figures.** Faces have distinct expressive eyes, eyebrows, smiles, and hairstyles.
2. **NEVER leave massive sterile empty white canvas.** Backgrounds always have rich ambient tone (warm parchment, dark chalkboard, starlit night, warm charcoal).
3. **NEVER hold a static visual for > 5.5 seconds** during normal narration.
4. **NEVER mix conflicting art universes** (e.g., photo + 3D + vector) in the same video.
5. **NEVER use complex distracting camera acrobatics** (3D spins, camera rolls, aggressive zooms).

---

## 13. Things HAY & ĐẸP. Currently Does Differently (and Gaps Identified)

1. **Asset Universe Consistency**: Current HAY & ĐẸP. catalog sometimes mixes stick-figure / flat corporate icons with watercolor cards. References strictly adhere to a unified, emotive 2D illustration universe.
2. **Atmospheric Cohesion**: References use full-screen ambient atmospheric layers (particles, warm parchment grain, soft vignette), whereas older HAY & ĐẸP. templates occasionally had a sterile background.
3. **Action Specificity**: Reference artwork depicts the *exact specific action* mentioned in the voiceover (e.g. "sửa từng thứ hỏng" -> drawing of man fixing a broken socket/lamp), rather than a generic family standing still.
